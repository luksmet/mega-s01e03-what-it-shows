#4471 was filed as one bug: the parser reads dates month first. Its reports hold two bugs with two different fixes. Each one has its own ticket, and engineering closed each one its own way.

#4471 was not one issue. The parser fix did not touch the booking-v3 reports.

The two problems

parser: the parser read month first. Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first. The shared parser read 07/04 as 4 July, so the day and the month swapped. This covers app, email and booking-v2 form reports. Engineering fixed it: the parser now reads dd/mm for EU locales.

hint: booking-v3 told people to type the month first. The form printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants followed it, and the form read their input day first. The date flipped before the parser saw it, so the parser fix did not reach it. Engineering will not fix the form: booking-v3 is retired.

One-day moves. A few parser reports describe a date that moved by one day, not a flip. The desk read them that way (desk-notes.json) and still files them under parser by tenant. If one-day moves come in after the parser fix, they are a time-zone bug: reopen the follow-up ticket instead of merging them into a closed one.

How the reports were placed

  • By tenant, not by intake tag. Intake guesses the tag from the reporter’s browser. The tenant’s locale and booking form decide the cause (causes.ts). The tags disagree with the tenant in both groups, so the table shows them only for reference.
  • By what happened, not by subject. Several subject lines appear under both problems. Matching on subject (decision 0001) cannot keep them apart.

Lessons

  • Before you merge a report into a big ticket, check how it came in and from which tenant, not only its subject line.
  • Count by id, and group by tenant, not by the intake tag.
